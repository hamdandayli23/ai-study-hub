Combinational Logic & Boolean Foundations

digital_systems_ch04.pdf · 28 Pages · Uploaded Today

Combinational logic represents the cornerstone of digital computing where the output state is strictly a mathematical function of current inputs, with zero dependence on previous clock cycles or internal storage registers.

Unlike sequential circuits (such as flip-flops, registers, and counters), combinational systems obey pure Boolean equations. Fundamental circuits analyzed in this chapter include half adders, full adders, multiplexers (MUX), demultiplexers, and priority encoders.

Key minimization techniques like Karnaugh Mapping (K-maps) and the Quine-McCluskey tabular algorithm eliminate redundant silicon gates to minimize propagation delay (τpd) and reduce dynamic switching power consumption.

What is the primary difference between a Half Adder and a Full Adder circuit, and how many inputs does each require?
Card 1 of 4
MODEL EXAM ANSWER Click Card to Flip 🔄
A Half Adder computes the sum of two 1-bit binary inputs (A, B) producing Sum = A ⊕ B and Carry = A · B. It has no input for a carry from previous stages.

A Full Adder takes three 1-bit inputs (A, B, and Carry-in $C_{in}$), allowing cascaded multi-bit addition.

DIGITAL SYSTEMS · CHAPTER 04

Which Boolean operation can be implemented using only two 2-input NAND gates?

CORRECT!

Connecting both inputs of a NAND gate together creates an inverter (NOT). Cascading two NAND gates produces an AND function.
